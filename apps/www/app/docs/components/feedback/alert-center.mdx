<PageHeader id="alert-center">Alert Center</PageHeader>

A single, top-level entry point for one-to-many account alerts — usage limits, hard limits, billing
problems — and the upgrade CTAs that resolve them. It replaces a stack of independent window banners with
**one** severity-colored bar that surfaces the highest-priority alert inline (its message _and_ its
call-to-action), plus a compact count-and-caret trigger that expands the remaining alerts as full-width banners,
ranked by severity.

Compose it into [`AppLayout.Notice`](/layouts/app-layout#applayoutnotice), the single top-of-window
composition slot for app-wide messaging. Alerts are **authored as JSX**: each `AlertCenter.Item` composes
the normal [`Alert`](/components/feedback/alert) banner parts as children and registers its coordination
facts (`id`, `intent`) with the center, which derives the count, the top alert, and the
severity ranking from the registrations. Mount an item to show its alert; unmount it to dismiss — dismissal
is just your state flipping a condition off. The demo runs in its own framed document — choose the
count-and-caret control to expand or collapse the full-width banners, or dismiss alerts (including the top
one) to watch the next-highest severity promote into the bar.

## Composition

Compose the parts of an `AlertCenter` together to build your own. Items render `null` in place — their
children are projected into the bar or an expansion row by rank:

```text showLineNumbers=false
AlertCenter.Root
├── AlertCenter.Bar        ← the top-ranked item's children, in Alert chrome
├── AlertCenter.Content    ← every other item's children, as ranked rows
└── AlertCenter.Item (0..n, renderless)
    └── Alert.Icon / Alert.Content / Alert.Title / Alert.Description /
        AlertCenter.DismissIconButton
```

## Polymorphism

Only one part takes `asChild`, and deliberately so:

- **`AlertCenter.DismissIconButton`** inherits it from the
  [IconButton](/components/actions/icon-button) it renders through
  [`Alert.DismissIconButton`](/components/feedback/alert#alertdismissiconbutton). Reach for it when dismissal
  is a link or a form submit rather than a click handler — the required `onClick` still runs, so keep the
  unmount there.

The rest have no `asChild` on purpose. `AlertCenter.Root` and `AlertCenter.Item` render no DOM at all, so
there is nothing to swap: `Root` is the state owner, and an `Item` registers its facts and projects its
children (see [Projection](#projection)). `AlertCenter.Bar` and `AlertCenter.Content` each render a fixed
structure with no single default element a swap could coherently replace — the bar is a presence wrapper that
owns the exit animation around an `Alert.Root` banner (swap the wrapper and the animation breaks; swap the
banner and the chrome the part exists to provide is gone), and the expansion is a focus-within container that
owns the collapse animation around the `<ul>` of rows. Both also take `children?: never`, since the content is
the registered items' projected children. The element a consumer actually wants to reach is each alert's
banner chrome, an `Alert.Root` whose parts you already author as that item's children.

## How it works

- **The bar surfaces the top alert inline.** `AlertCenter.Bar` renders the highest-severity item's authored
  children — icon, title, description, and call-to-action — directly in the strip, so the most urgent CTA is
  one glance and zero extra clicks away. Hiding _every_ CTA behind a click is exactly what makes banners fail
  to convert.
- **The count-and-caret control expands the full list.** When two or more alerts are active, the bar renders
  a compact control that expands `AlertCenter.Content` beneath it. The additional alerts render as compact,
  full-width one- or two-line banners, push the app layout down, and can be collapsed by the same control.
- **Content is composed; facts are registered.** Each `AlertCenter.Item` renders no DOM where it's
  authored — it registers `{ id, intent }` and portals its children into a stable host the bar or an
  expansion row adopts (see [Projection](#projection)). You get real anchors, router links, stateful and
  context-aware JSX in every banner, while the count, the top alert, and the ordering stay derived and
  deterministic.
- **Ranking is severity, then arrival.** `danger` › `warning` › `important` › `info` › `success`; within an
  intent, alerts keep the order they first appeared — items mounting together rank in authored order, later
  arrivals append after their same-intent peers, and a dismissed alert that returns resumes its original
  position.
- **It collapses when empty.** With no items mounted the bar animates out and renders nothing, mirroring
  `AppLayout.Notice`, so `Root`, `Bar`, and `Content` can all stay mounted unconditionally.
- **It announces politely.** A persistent, visually-hidden `role="status"` live region (mounted by
  `AlertCenter.Root`) announces the top alert and how many more as alerts arrive or re-rank — a live region
  must exist before its text changes to be announced reliably. Its headline is the top alert's **rendered**
  `Alert.Title` text (inline CTA anchors stripped), re-read on every commit so the announcement stays in
  lockstep with the visible copy. The bar itself claims no ARIA `banner` landmark.

## Basic usage

Author one `AlertCenter.Item` per alert, composing the `Alert` banner parts as its children. The consumer
owns dismissal (including any persistence, e.g. `sessionStorage`): compose an
`AlertCenter.DismissIconButton` whose `onClick` unmounts the item — dismissing the top alert promotes the
next-highest severity into the bar. An item authored without one (say, a hard-stop billing failure the user
must resolve) is simply not dismissable — see [Dismissability](#dismissability) for choosing which alerts
offer it.

## Dismissability

Dismissability is composition, not configuration: an alert can be dismissed exactly when its children
include an `AlertCenter.DismissIconButton`. Choose per alert, with the intent as your default policy:

- **`danger` alerts should generally not be dismissable.** They represent hard-stop conditions — a failed
  payment, an expired certificate — that only resolving the underlying problem should clear. A dismissable
  danger alert trains users to swat away the one banner that genuinely blocks them.
- **`warning` and `info` alerts should generally be dismissable.** They are acknowledgeable by nature —
  usage nags, rollout notices — and a nag that cannot be acknowledged erodes trust in the whole bar.
- Both are best practices, not constraints the API enforces — dismissability stays a per-alert composition
  choice in either direction. A warning that describes an active blockage (a hard limit already being
  enforced) can withhold the dismiss button like a danger alert, and a danger alert can still compose one
  when your product calls for it — the framed shell demo at the top of this page does exactly that, so you
  can watch the top slot re-rank when its danger alert is dismissed.

Because the bar always surfaces the top-ranked alert, a non-dismissable danger alert keeps the bar pinned
to the most urgent call-to-action while acknowledgeable alerts come and go beneath it. This demo opens
expanded — note the top danger alert and the hard-limit warning render no dismiss button:

## Persisting dismissals

Dismissal is your state, so persistence is your choice of store. Session scope is usually the right default
for acknowledgements — [`useSessionStorage`](/hooks#usesessionstorage) keeps a dismissal for the rest of the
tab session (a reload doesn't resurrect acknowledged nags) while a fresh session starts clean.

Most real alert bars render **server state**: the backend reports the currently-true conditions on every
fetch, and the client authors one `AlertCenter.Item` per condition. Two rules make dismissal behave when the
same alerts keep arriving as state:

- **Key dismissals on stable condition ids.** Use the id of the _condition_ (`"transfer-limit"`), not a
  per-occurrence UUID — otherwise every refetch mints a "new" alert and dismissal never sticks. Filter the
  server's list against the dismissed set before mounting items: the server keeps reporting the condition,
  and the client keeps suppressing it.
- **Prune dismissals the server no longer reports.** When a condition clears server-side and later recurs,
  it is a new occurrence the user hasn't acknowledged — drop stale ids from the dismissed set whenever the
  server list changes, so recurrences resurface instead of staying silenced forever. (If your backend
  versions occurrences — say a `raisedAt` timestamp — key dismissals on `id:raisedAt` and skip the pruning.)
  Thanks to sticky ranking, a returning condition resumes its original position within its intent tier.

The demo simulates the fetch loop: dismiss the transfer warning, then resolve and re-raise the condition on
the "server" — the alert returns even though its id was acknowledged, because the dismissal was pruned when
the condition cleared. Reload the page mid-session and acknowledged alerts stay away.

</Example>

```tsx
import { Alert } from "@ngrok/mantle/alert";
import type { AlertCenterIntent } from "@ngrok/mantle/alert-center";
import { AlertCenter } from "@ngrok/mantle/alert-center";
import { useSessionStorage } from "@ngrok/mantle/hooks";
import { useEffect, useMemo } from "react";

type ServerAlert = {
	id: string;
	intent: AlertCenterIntent;
	title: string;
	cta: string;
	href: string;
};

/** Decode the sessionStorage entry (a JSON array of alert ids); corrupt entries mean "nothing dismissed". */
function parseDismissedIds(raw: string): ReadonlyArray<string> {
	try {
		const parsed: unknown = JSON.parse(raw);
		if (!Array.isArray(parsed)) {
			return [];
		}
		return parsed.filter((entry): entry is string => typeof entry === "string");
	} catch {
		return [];
	}
}

export function AccountAlerts({ serverAlerts }: { serverAlerts: ReadonlyArray<ServerAlert> }) {
	const [dismissedRaw, setDismissedRaw] = useSessionStorage("dismissed-alerts", "[]");
	const dismissed = useMemo(() => new Set(parseDismissedIds(dismissedRaw)), [dismissedRaw]);
	const dismiss = (id: string) => setDismissedRaw(JSON.stringify([...dismissed, id]));

	// Reconcile: a dismissal only suppresses the occurrence the server is still
	// reporting. When a condition clears server-side, forget its dismissal so a
	// future recurrence resurfaces.
	useEffect(() => {
		const activeIds = new Set(serverAlerts.map((alert) => alert.id));
		const pruned = [...dismissed].filter((id) => activeIds.has(id));
		if (pruned.length !== dismissed.size) {
			setDismissedRaw(JSON.stringify(pruned));
		}
	}, [serverAlerts, dismissed, setDismissedRaw]);

	return (
		<AlertCenter.Root>
			<AlertCenter.Bar />
			<AlertCenter.Content />
			{serverAlerts
				.filter((alert) => !dismissed.has(alert.id))
				.map((alert) => (
					<AlertCenter.Item key={alert.id} id={alert.id} intent={alert.intent}>
						<Alert.Icon />
						<Alert.Content>
							<Alert.Title>
								{alert.title} <a href={alert.href}>{alert.cta}</a>
							</Alert.Title>
							{/* this app's dismissability policy, applied in one place: its danger alerts don't offer dismissal */}
							{alert.intent !== "danger" && (
								<AlertCenter.DismissIconButton onClick={() => dismiss(alert.id)} />
							)}
						</Alert.Content>
					</AlertCenter.Item>
				))}
		</AlertCenter.Root>
	);
}
```

a single line for a title alone and a two-line banner once a description is composed, exactly like the same
alert renders as an expansion row. Nothing is suppressed by placement, so a CTA reads naturally from the

Two things still argue for a title on every alert, and for keeping bar copy short:

- **The title is the announcement.** The live region headlines with the top alert's rendered `Alert.Title`
  (CTA anchors stripped); a description is not announced. An alert with no title announces as a bare count.
- **The bar pushes the app shell down.** It is the topmost thing in the window, so every line it grows costs
  the page. Detail that only matters once someone is reading the full list belongs in the expansion: the
  chrome around your children carries `data-placement="bar"` in the bar and `data-placement="list"` in the
  expansion, so `in-data-[placement=bar]:hidden` on any element — a description included — keeps it out of
  the bar and shows it only in a row.

## Projection

`AlertCenter.Item` renders no DOM where it's authored; its children render through a portal into a stable
per-item host element that the `Bar`/`Content` chrome physically adopts (typically inside
`AppLayout.Notice`). The children's _React tree_ stays where the item is written — only their DOM moves —
which is what makes the projection behave like ordinary composition:

- **Context and events flow from the authoring site.** Providers and error boundaries wrapping an
  individual item work, router links resolve against the route that renders the item, and the children's
  React events (clicks, focus) bubble to the author's tree — a wrapper around one item observes only its
  own alert.
- **Content keeps its state across re-ranks.** The host element is moved between the bar and a row — never
  remounted — so component state in your alert content survives promotions and demotions. In engines with
  `Element.moveBefore` even focus and CSS transitions survive the move; elsewhere the center's focus
  redirects re-establish keyboard position.
- **Don't compose `Alert.Root` in children.** The center provides the banner chrome — with the item's
  `intent` — at whichever placement the item ranks into. Children are the banner's _content_: `Alert.Icon`,
  `Alert.Content`, `Alert.Title`, `Alert.Description`, `AlertCenter.DismissIconButton`.
- **Ids identify the projection, so they must be unique.** Both placements adopt the one stable host an id
  owns, so a second _mounted_ item claiming an already-registered id throws rather than silently stealing the
  first item's content. Unmounting and remounting the same id is fine — that is what dismissal and a
  server-reported condition recurring are, and the returning alert resumes its original position.

The chrome around your children is also a styling hook. It carries `data-placement="bar"` in the bar and
`data-placement="list"` in an expansion row (plus `data-alert-id` for the alert it holds), so
`in-data-[placement=bar]:hidden` on any element inside an item's children keeps that element out of the bar
and shows it only in a row — and `in-data-[placement=list]:hidden` does the reverse.

A mounted item's children always render (React-wise), even while they rank nowhere visible — e.g. below
the bar with no expansion composed, where the host simply stays detached from the document. Because items
register from effects, avoid authoring them inside `Suspense` boundaries that can hide them (hiding
unregisters the alert and the bar animates out), and server-rendered HTML contains no bar — it enters with
its height animation after hydration, the same entrance every arriving alert gets by design.

### Where `AlertCenter.Root` goes

Every other example on this page puts `AlertCenter.Root` inside `AppLayout.Notice` with its items as
siblings, which is all a **shell-authored** alert needs. But projection's whole point is that a **route** can
author an alert — and `AlertCenter.Item` throws outside a `Root`, so for that to work `Root` has to be an
ancestor of whatever renders your routes.

So wrap the shell, and leave only the chrome in the notice slot. Any route below can then raise an alert into
the shell's bar by rendering an item — no shell state, no prop drilling, no context of your own. Choose
**Endpoints** to have the route raise a DNS warning that outranks the account notice, then **Agents** to
watch it clear:

```tsx
						<Alert.Content>
							<Alert.Title>
								New region available: eu-west <a href="/regions">Learn more</a>
							</Alert.Title>
						</Alert.Content>
					</AlertCenter.Item>
				</AppLayout.Notice>
				<AppLayout.Workspace>
					<AppLayout.Content>
						<AppLayout.Header>
							{routes.map((route) => (
								<Button
									key={route.path}
									type="button"
									appearance={pathname === route.path ? "filled" : "outlined"}
									intent="neutral"
									size="sm"
									onClick={() => setPathname(route.path)}
								>
									{route.label}
								</Button>
							))}
						</AppLayout.Header>
						<AppLayout.Body>
							{pathname === "/endpoints" ? <EndpointsRoute /> : <AgentsRoute />}
						</AppLayout.Body>
					</AppLayout.Content>
				</AppLayout.Workspace>
			</AppLayout.Root>
		</AlertCenter.Root>
	);
}
```

The item unmounts with the route, so the alert clears on navigation for free. The route buttons stand in for
a router — a real shell renders its `Outlet` inside `AppLayout.Body`, pins the shell with
`className="fixed inset-0"`, and composes the `Main` landmark onto `AppLayout.Body` with `asChild`, exactly
as in the [example at the top of this page](#alert-center). This demo is embedded in a docs page that already
owns the document, so it does neither.

One consequence worth knowing: `AlertCenter.Root` renders the `sr-only` live region at its **own** position
in the tree, so hoisting it also moves that element out of the notice slot. It is visually hidden either way,
and it must stay mounted to announce anything, so this is harmless — but it is why `Root` is renderless rather
than a wrapper you can style.

## Testing

**An alert's title is in the accessibility tree more than once, by design.** `AlertCenter.Root` mounts an
`sr-only` polite live region that headlines with the top alert's title, and the bar renders that same title
visibly — and if the item composes an `AlertCenter.DismissIconButton`, its accessible name (`"Dismiss
&lt;title&gt;"`) is a third copy. None of them is `aria-hidden`: a polite region has to pre-exist in the tree to
announce anything, and hiding the visible title would break the bar.

So `getByText(/Payment failed/)` throws `Found multiple elements`. **Scope the query instead of matching text
globally.** Four patterns cover everything:

```tsx
// 1. Assert the bar's content by scoping to its data-slot
const bar = container.querySelector('[data-slot="alert-center-bar"]');
expect(bar).toContainElement(screen.getByRole("heading", { level: 5, name: /Payment failed/ }));

// 2. Or assert text on the element itself rather than querying the document
expect(bar).toHaveTextContent("Payment failed");

// 3. Read the live region through its role, with an ANCHORED regex — an
//    unanchored one also matches the "Payment failed, and 2 more alerts" form
expect(screen.getByRole("status")).toHaveTextContent(/^Payment failed$/);
expect(screen.getByRole("status")).toHaveAttribute("aria-live", "polite");

// 4. Reach controls by role + accessible name
await user.click(screen.getByRole("button", { name: "Dismiss Payment failed" }));
```

`getByText` is still fine for a **description**, which the live region never contains — it headlines with the
title only.

Two more things worth knowing when writing these tests:

- The live region's text is derived from the bar's rendered DOM, not from your props, so it settles in an
  effect. Assert it with `await waitFor(...)` if you are checking it immediately after a state change.
- Use `data-alert-id` to target one specific alert when several are registered, and `data-placement` to
  assert _where_ an item's children ended up (`bar` vs `list`) rather than inferring it from ordering.

## API Reference

### AlertCenter.Root

The renderless state owner. It creates the registration store the items register into, owns the expanded
state of the inline list, and mounts the persistent `role="status"` live region. Compose its `Bar` and
`Content` inside `AppLayout.Notice`, alongside any other window-level notice.

| Prop           | Type                      | Default | Description                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `open`         | `boolean`                 | —       | Controlled expanded state of the additional-alert list. Pair with `onOpenChange`.                                                                                                        |
| `defaultOpen`  | `boolean`                 | `false` | Uncontrolled initial expanded state of the additional-alert list.                                                                                                                        |
| `onOpenChange` | `(open: boolean) => void` | —       | Called when the additional-alert list expands or collapses.                                                                                                                              |
| `children`     | `ReactNode`               | —       | The `Bar`, the `Content`, and every `Item`. Items may be authored anywhere under the root — their children stay in this tree and are projected into the bar or an expansion row by rank. |

`Root` renders no DOM of its own beyond the live region it mounts:

| Data Attribute | Value                    | Description                                                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-slot`    | `alert-center-announcer` | On the persistent visually-hidden `role="status"` live region Root mounts. |

### AlertCenter.Bar

The always-visible, full-width strip. It renders the highest-severity item's children inside a banner
`Alert` chrome (with that item's `intent`), appends the count-and-caret expansion control when more alerts
exist **and an `AlertCenter.Content` is composed** (without one there is nothing to expand, so no control
renders), and collapses to nothing when there are no alerts. It claims no ARIA `banner` landmark.

When the top alert changes while keyboard focus was on a control that the change removed (dismissing alert
after alert), focus is redirected to the new bar's first trailing control instead of dropping to `<body>`;
a control that survives the swap keeps focus.

All props from [div](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Element/div#attributes) are forwarded
to the banner chrome. `appearance` and `intent` are the component's own: the chrome is always a `banner`
`Alert.Root` carrying the top item's `intent`.

| Prop        | Type                  | Default | Description                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `className` | `string`              | —       | Merged onto the banner chrome after the bar's own classes, so yours win. The presence wrapper that animates the bar in and out is not stylable. |
| `data-slot` | `string`              | —       | Joined ancestors-first with `alert-center-bar` on the chrome, so a wrapping component can add its own slot without dropping this one.           |
| `ref`       | `Ref<HTMLDivElement>` | —       | Forwarded to the banner chrome, not to the presence wrapper.                                                                                    |
| `children`  | `never`               | —       | Not accepted — the bar renders the top-ranked item's authored children.                                                                         |

| Data Attribute       | Value                          | Description                                                                                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-slot`          | `alert-center-bar-wrapper`     | On the presence wrapper that owns the enter/exit animation.                                  |
| `data-slot`          | `alert-center-bar`             | On the banner chrome (`Alert.Root`) the top item's children render inside.                   |
| `data-slot`          | `alert-center-bar-mount`       | On the layout-transparent element the top item's host is adopted into.                       |
| `data-state`         | `"open"` \| `"closed"`         | On the wrapper; drives the enter/exit transition, and stays `closed` while the exit plays.   |
| `data-placement`     | `"bar"`                        | On the chrome — where the top item's children are currently rendering.                       |
| `data-alert-id`      | the top item's `id`            | On the chrome; per-alert styling and testing hook.                                           |
| `data-alert-dismiss` | present on the dismiss control | Read, not stamped — `Alert` emits it; the bar's CSS centers and offsets the control with it. |
| `data-alert-expand`  | present on the expand control  | Read, not stamped — `Alert` emits it; the bar's CSS centers the control with it.             |

### AlertCenter.Content

The inline expansion beneath the bar. It lists every alert hidden by the bar, ranked
highest-severity-first, as full-width banner rows. It renders only when additional alerts exist.

All props from [div](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Element/div#attributes) except
`children` and `id` — `id` is reserved, since it anchors the expand control's `aria-controls` — plus:

| Prop              | Type                  | Default         | Description                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `aria-label`      | `string`              | `"More alerts"` | Names the row list (`<ul>`). The wrapper around it is `role="generic"`, where ARIA prohibits an accessible name, so the label lands on the list itself. |
| `aria-labelledby` | `string`              | —               | Names the row list from another element's id; when set, `aria-label` is not applied.                                                                    |
| `className`       | `string`              | —               | Merged onto the expansion wrapper after the collapse-animation classes.                                                                                 |
| `data-slot`       | `string`              | —               | Joined ancestors-first with `alert-center-content` on the wrapper.                                                                                      |
| `ref`             | `Ref<HTMLDivElement>` | —               | Forwarded to the expansion wrapper.                                                                                                                     |
| `children`        | `never`               | —               | Not accepted — the rows are the registered items' authored children.                                                                                    |

| Data Attribute     | Value                  | Description                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-slot`        | `alert-center-content` | On the expansion wrapper that owns the collapse animation.                                  |
| `data-slot`        | `alert-center-item`    | On each row's banner chrome (`Alert.Root`).                                                 |
| `data-state`       | `"open"` \| `"closed"` | On the wrapper; drives the collapse transition.                                             |
| `data-placement`   | `"list"`               | On each row's chrome — where that item's children are currently rendering.                  |
| `data-alert-id`    | the row item's `id`    | On each row's chrome; per-alert styling and testing hook.                                   |
| `data-alert-mount` | the row item's `id`    | On the row's layout-transparent mount point — the element that item's host is adopted into. |

### AlertCenter.Item

One authored alert: the coordination facts as props, the banner content as children. Renders `null` in
place and registers with the center; mount to show, unmount to dismiss. Two items mounted at once may not
share an `id` — the second registration throws, since both would project into that id's single host.

| Prop        | Type                                                          | Default | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`        | `string`                                                      | —       | Stable identity: keys the projection, pins arrival order (a returning id resumes its spot), and stamps `data-alert-id`. Unique among the items mounted under one `AlertCenter.Root`; unmounting and remounting the same id is fine. |
| `intent`    | `"danger" \| "important" \| "info" \| "success" \| "warning"` | —       | The tone the alert communicates. Drives severity ranking and the banner chrome's intent.                                                                                                                                            |
| `className` | `string`                                                      | —       | Optional classes for this item's chrome `Alert.Root`, in both placements.                                                                                                                                                           |
| `children`  | `ReactNode`                                                   | —       | The banner content — `Alert` parts, not `Alert.Root`. Rendered at the Bar/Content position, not where the item is authored.                                                                                                         |

| Data Attribute    | Value                    | Description                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `data-slot`       | `alert-center-item-host` | On the stable per-id host element the item's children portal into. |
| `data-alert-host` | the item's `id`          | On that same host — which alert's projected children it holds.     |

### AlertCenter.DismissIconButton

The per-item dismiss affordance: a thin wrapper over
[`Alert.DismissIconButton`](/components/feedback/alert#alertdismissiconbutton) that defaults its `label` to
`Dismiss ${title}` — derived from the enclosing banner's **rendered** `Alert.Title` text with inline CTA
anchors stripped and re-read on every commit, so the accessible name stays in lockstep with the visible
copy. Compose it — or leave it out — per alert; its presence is what makes that alert dismissable. Where it
sits is the chrome's business: the bar centers its trailing controls while it is a single line and
top-aligns them once the top alert has a description, matching every expansion row.

All props from [Alert.DismissIconButton](/components/feedback/alert#alertdismissiconbutton), plus:

| Prop        | Type                | Default            | Description                                                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `onClick`   | `MouseEventHandler` | —                  | Required. Unmount the item (update your state); the center re-ranks from what remains. |
| `label`     | `string`            | `Dismiss ${title}` | Accessible name override — e.g. when the rendered title is long.                       |
| `data-slot` | `string`            | —                  | Joined ancestors-first with `alert-center-dismiss-icon-button` on the rendered button. |

| Data Attribute       | Value                              | Description                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `data-slot`          | `alert-center-dismiss-icon-button` | Replaces `Alert.DismissIconButton`'s own `alert-dismiss-icon-button` on the rendered button.     |
| `data-alert-dismiss` | present                            | Read, not stamped — `Alert` emits it, and `AlertCenter`'s bar CSS positions the control with it. |
